Relating a new plumbing symptom to work by other trades

A new drip, stain, noise or slow drain may follow flooring, cabinet, drywall, electrical or landscaping work without being caused by it; the sequence is context, not a verdict. Before you send

What to Photograph Before You Send a Request

Clear photos help a provider understand the job before anyone visits. Keep them handy for the follow-up call or email.

  • Water heater: the rating plate, the vent pipe where it leaves the tank, and the rental sticker or contract if there is one.
  • Blocked drain: the fixtures that drain slowly or back up, whether it's only the lowest level, and the floor drain or cleanout if it's accessible.
  • Leak: the stain or damp patch, the water meter dial with every tap closed, and any recent renovation nearby.
  • Sump pump: the pit with the lid off, where the discharge pipe leaves the house, and any battery or water-powered backup.
  • Older supply pipe: the pipe beside the water meter. Copper looks orange-brown, galvanized steel is grey and threaded, and lead is dull grey and turns shiny silver where you scratch it with a key.
FAQ

Questions About Your Pembroke Request

Is it fine to put a stain and a slow drain in a single Pembroke request?

Yes, if both appeared after the same other work: describe each symptom separately and note the dates of the work they may relate to. Unrelated items, such as a worn tap and a softener question, are clearer as separate requests. Our enquiry team reviews what you send and a provider decides how to scope it.

Does a new leak after renovations mean the other trades caused it?

No. A symptom that appears after other work may be related, unrelated or both, and the timing alone does not decide it. Give the sequence of work and the first-noticed date as context, and ask the respondent to describe what they observe rather than who is responsible. Questions about responsibility belong with the parties involved, or an insurer or lawyer, not with the assessment.

What should I send for my Pembroke home when a symptom followed other work?

Send a dated list of the other work, note where screws, cuts or heavy items were placed near pipes or equipment, give the day you first saw the symptom and add any earlier and later photographs. Name the fixture or equipment affected, who can authorize work and who can open the door. Keep guesses about the cause out of it.

After I send details of a symptom and the other work, is a provider lined up for my Pembroke address?

No. A request is only a description of what you need. Our enquiry team reviews it, and any provider must separately confirm the address, the work, their qualifications, the written scope, pricing and scheduling before anything is agreed or booked. Coverage and availability stay unconfirmed until that happens. Sending alone commits you to nothing.

How can I describe the other trades' work in Pembroke without blaming anyone?

State what was done, by which trade and on which dates, using neutral facts such as photos, receipts and messages. Use plain words such as moved, cut or refitted rather than damaged or careless, and leave out guesses about fault. The respondent is asked to assess the symptom, not to settle who is responsible.

What if I have no photos from before the other work started?

A request is still useful without them. Describe what you remember, such as where a cabinet now stands or which wall was opened, and add current photos of the fixture, shutoff and any stain. Receipts, messages with the other trades and calendar entries can also help fix the dates. Say plainly which details are remembered and which are documented.
